# iPhone Astrophotography Camera

## 1. Product Vision

Build a professional iPhone camera application specifically optimized for astrophotography.

The app should combine:

1. Manual camera controls
2. Long-exposure capture
3. RAW/ProRAW acquisition where supported
4. Multi-frame stacking
5. Computational noise reduction
6. Exposure and color correction
7. Star-preserving enhancement
8. Automatic astrophotography-specific image processing

The goal is to produce significantly better night-sky photographs than the standard iPhone camera while keeping the result natural and preserving actual stars.

---

# 2. Primary Use Cases

### A. Milky Way photography

Capture the Milky Way with foreground objects and landscape.

### B. Star-field photography

Capture maximum numbers of visible stars with minimal noise.

### C. Night landscape

Capture a bright foreground together with a detailed sky.

### D. Moon + stars

Provide specialized exposure handling for scenes containing the Moon.

### E. Star trails

Capture a sequence of exposures and combine them into a continuous star-trail image.

### F. Deep-sky experimentation

Allow advanced users to capture many short/medium exposures for stacking.

# 11. Multi-Frame Stacking

This is the core computational feature.

For N captured frames:

```text
Frame 1 ─┐
Frame 2 ─┤
Frame 3 ─┤
Frame 4 ─┼── Alignment ── Stacking
...      ┤
Frame N ─┘
```

Before stacking:

1. Detect stars
2. Determine frame translation
3. Estimate rotation
4. Reject corrupted frames
5. Correct lens distortion
6. Normalize exposure
7. Align stars

Then stack using robust statistical methods.

Possible algorithms:

* Mean stacking
* Median stacking
* Sigma clipping
* Weighted averaging

Default:

**Weighted sigma-clipped stacking**

because it can suppress random noise while retaining stars.

---

# 12. Star Alignment

The app must distinguish between:

### Stars

which should remain aligned,

and

### Ground

which may move relative to the stars because of Earth's rotation.

This enables two important modes.

## Sky Mode

Align frames based on stars.

Best for:

* Milky Way
* Star fields
* Deep sky

## Landscape Mode

Keep the ground fixed while processing the sky separately.

Best for:

* Milky Way + mountains
* Buildings + stars
* Trees + sky

---

# 13. Sky/Ground Processing

For landscape astrophotography, process the image as two regions:

```text
             SKY
 ┌─────────────────────────┐
 │  ✦     ✦       ✦       │
 │     ✦      ✦           │
 │ ✦       Milky Way      │
 ├─────────────────────────┤
 │                         │
 │       LANDSCAPE         │
 │                         │
 └─────────────────────────┘
```

Sky:

* Align to stars
* Stack aggressively
* Increase signal
* Reduce noise

Ground:

* Keep geometrically stable
* Use lower-noise exposure
* Preserve natural textures

Then blend the two.

The transition should be automatically detected but manually adjustable.

---

# 14. Noise Reduction

Astrophotography noise should not be treated like ordinary photography noise.

Separate:

* Photon noise
* Read noise
* Hot pixels
* Fixed-pattern noise
* Color noise
* Thermal noise

Processing pipeline:

```text
RAW
 ↓
Black-level correction
 ↓
Hot-pixel detection
 ↓
Bad-pixel correction
 ↓
Frame stacking
 ↓
Chrominance noise reduction
 ↓
Luminance noise reduction
```

Avoid excessive smoothing.

Stars must remain point-like.

---

# 15. Hot Pixel Removal

Long exposures can create bright fixed pixels.

The app should identify pixels that remain at the same sensor coordinates across frames.

If:

```text
Pixel X = bright
Frame 1 = bright
Frame 2 = bright
Frame 3 = bright
Frame 4 = bright
```

and its location does not move with the stars, classify it as a sensor artifact.

Remove it before final processing.

---

# 16. Dark Frame Calibration

Advanced mode should support dark frames.

User can capture:

**Dark Frame**

with:

```text
Lens covered
Same ISO
Same shutter
Same temperature
```

The app can subtract the sensor noise pattern.

Example:

```text
Light Frame
      -
Dark Frame
      =
Calibrated Frame
```

For ordinary users, the app should automatically maintain reusable calibration profiles when practical.

---

# 17. Brightness Recovery

After stacking, the image will often appear extremely dark.

Use a controlled tone-processing pipeline:

```text
Linear RAW
 ↓
Black point adjustment
 ↓
Exposure recovery
 ↓
White balance
 ↓
Contrast
 ↓
Local contrast
 ↓
Highlight protection
 ↓
Shadow recovery
 ↓
Tone mapping
```

Do not simply increase brightness.

The goal is to recover faint astronomical structures while preserving natural contrast.

# 38. Memory Management

Long sessions can generate hundreds or thousands of frames.

Never keep all full-resolution frames uncompressed in RAM.

Use:

```text
Capture
 ↓
Temporary storage
 ↓
Tile-based processing
 ↓
Accumulation buffer
 ↓
Discard processed source frame
```

Use a persistent session directory.

# 43. Important Image-Processing Principle

The app must follow one fundamental rule:

**Enhance information that exists in the captured data; do not invent astronomical detail.**

Avoid:

* Artificial stars
* Fake Milky Way structures
* Excessive sharpening
* Plastic-looking noise reduction
* Over-saturated stars
* AI-generated sky replacement

The application should produce images that remain believable when zoomed to 100%.

# 47. Success Criteria

The application should outperform the standard iPhone night camera specifically in:

### Stars

More visible stars with less noise.

### Milky Way

Higher contrast and better color separation.

### Noise

Lower chroma and luminance noise.

### Detail

More recoverable faint detail.

### Dynamic range

Better balance between sky and foreground.

### Naturalness

No obvious computational artifacts.

The most important benchmark is not simply:

**"Does the image look brighter?"**

It is:

**"Can the user see significantly more genuine astronomical information than in a single standard iPhone photograph?"**
